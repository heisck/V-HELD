# Navigation Design Standards

## Purpose & Principles

Do not simply drop in a generic, cookie-cutter navbar. Invent navigation that feels integrated into the world of V-HELD (warm, inviting, community-grounded, professional NGO interface).

---

## Navigation Architecture

Based on V-HELD's project requirements ([docs/project-details.md](project-details.md)), the core navigation routes are:
1. **Home**
2. **About V-HELD**
3. **Volunteer With Us**
4. **Our Programmes** (Education, Health, Leadership Development, Community)
5. **Our Impact**
6. **Stories**
7. **Partner With Us**
8. **Support Our Work**
9. **Contact**
10. **Primary Action**: **APPLY TO VOLUNTEER**

---

## Usability Over Novelty

While navigation should feel custom, tactile, and thoughtfully designed:
* **Never sacrifice usability for novelty.**
* Users must immediately understand:
  - Where they are at all times (clear active states, breadcrumbs/indicators).
  - Where they can go next.
  - How to return effortlessly to previous views or the home view.
  - What sections exist across the site.
* On mobile and touch devices:
  - Accessible, ergonomic navigation drawer/sheet.
  - High-contrast, easy-to-tap targets (minimum 44 × 44px).
  - Full keyboard focusability (Tab, Shift+Tab, Escape to close).
